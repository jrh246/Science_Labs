## Learning design and source review

Question: How does detergent change the amount of liquid a coin can hold?

- Controlled comparison: student records a prediction/design before apparatus instructions, cleans a coin, places the correct dedicated dropper, observes first overflow and interprets retained count/volume. The first prediction is preserved and need not match the model result.
- Mechanism: visible dome growth/spill, two concept checks, and original writing connect cohesion and surface tension with molecular attractions and detergent action. Water stays H2O; surface tension is not a solid film. A surfactant changes interactions at the surface.
- Limits: student explains matching drop size, coin face/cleanliness, height and temperature, and distinguishes an indirect comparison from numerical surface-tension measurement or repeatability evidence.

Sources read: American Chemical Society, https://www.acs.org/middleschoolchemistry/lessonplans/chapter5/lesson2.html and https://www.acs.org/education/whatischemistry/adventures-in-chemistry/experiments/surface-spectaculars.html. The sources support water domes, molecular attractions and detergent effects. They do not supply our exact virtual counts. USGS search corroborated cohesion; direct page retrieval timed out, so the direct reviewed teaching sources are ACS.

Two conditions answer different questions. One run per liquid is sufficient for this teaching comparison; it cannot estimate repeatability. Three individual additions show growth and establish the drop-size method. Optional five-drop batches replace repetitive motions, stop at first overflow, and are explicitly model-provided rather than independent trials. The student still interprets the counter and enters the last retained amount and its volume. Real coin/detergent behavior depends on more factors; synthetic thresholds, enlarged artwork and simplified fluid behavior are clearly identified.
