# Soil water — individual build brief

Question: How do three selected soil models differ in drainage and retention, and does all remaining water support root uptake?

Three goals: control a water-budget comparison; read collected drainage and calculate retained water; distinguish total retained water from a supplied potentially plant-available portion. Misconceptions: more drainate means more retention; the wettest soil must provide most usable water; one short drainage reading measures field capacity, drainage speed, root uptake or every soil of that texture.

Three distinct model compositions, not three repeated trials: sand-dominant, loam mixture of sand/silt/clay, clay-dominant. Equal100cm³ initially dry bulk cores, ideal50mL pore volume, matched column/ideal nonabsorbing support,60mL dose and defined complete model gravity-drainage endpoint. No claim that equal volumes imply equal dry masses or that real porosities/structure are identical. All chosen numerical values synthetic, not sourced field data. Endpoint drainate45/30/20mL yields retention15/30/40mL. Separate supplied hypothetical tightly held amounts5/10/25mL yield potentially available10/20/15mL. That partition cannot be determined from drainage alone. No physical wilting experiment, soil-handling or extraction protocol.

Required prediction and control plan precede observations. Move a collector under each prepacked column, place a measured60mL doser at the actual column mouth, then finish its one model free-drainage step. Intermediate synthetic budget is50mL in pores/10mL collected; final drainate+retention remains60mL. It is an idealized staged model, not a time-resolved flow law. No real minutes or measured speed claimed. Read actual provided cylinder/meniscus values with equivalent text; calculate each retention and actual rankings. Three one-per-composition runs add contrasts, no default triplicate prep. Supplied prepacked cores/supports streamline low-learning preparation.

Later place gravity/root-available/tightly-held interpretation cards on an original enlarged pore/particle/root diagram and calculate potentially available water using the clearly separate supplied test data. Root diagram not a measured specimen, molecule-size or exact three-compartment map. Water is in pores/films, not absorbed into solid grains. Air after drainage matters for root oxygen; potential water availability does not establish whole-plant growth or best soil.

Institutional sources actually read: UC IPM Soil Properties and Water Availability to Roots; UMN How Agricultural Drainage Works soil-water/field-capacity/wilting/drainage sections; USDA NRCS2008 Available Water Capacity two-page text. NRCS text references a figure; web screenshot produced a reference without visible image, so no figure inspection claim. Source qualitative relationships do not validate our coefficients/volumes. Do not teach loam as equal thirds or organic matter as a mineral texture class; do not copy conflicting particle cutoffs from general text.

Completed PDF/native print/JSON must retain current wrong/empty readings, calculations, supplied-data source, first plan, interpretations, meaningful corrections and original writing. Optional identity and truthful storage feedback; English versioned IDs prepared for future translation.
