# Map your visual blind spot — individual build brief

Question: how do reported target visibility and eye condition relate to the optic disc, and what can a coarse screen map establish? Three goals: retinal photoreceptors versus optic-disc/nerve output; interpret a one-dimensional visibility profile and both-eyes comparison; explain fixation, filling-in, sampling and physical-scale limits. No clinical vision assessment or exact retinal area/diameter.

Two explicit modes chosen before prediction: own reported perception, or analyze an illustrative supplied example. Never programmatically hide/fade the target when a student reports not seeing it. Always-drawn dot on plain high-contrast background, fixation cross, direct horizontal drag/range/keyboard positioning, re-fixate cross before reporting visible/not-seen/uncertain. Keep comfortable viewing position/eye-to-screen distance, device/zoom/setup stable; do not require finding a gap or force personal perception to match a model. Right-eye target to right, left-eye target to left; one both-eyes comparison, preferably at a previously reported missing point. These are distinct eye conditions/spatial sample points, not default repeated trials or independent participants.

Own-mode minimum three distinct positions per eye within at least one recorded matching screen-width/scale context, plus one both-eyes point. At least three points can sample before/inside/after a localized gap, but no gap is a valid result. More positions only if needed; preserve prior contexts/points and current revisions. Each report keeps actual displayed separation in CSS/browser pixels, measured panel width/scale and self-reported eye/fixation/context. No pixel-to-cm/degree/retinal conversion without calibration. Resizing changes the current target geometry but never regenerates old records. Gaze, head distance and eye closure are not tracked/verified. Changes of setup limit comparisons.

Example-mode data deliberately illustrative, not a human dataset: right/left eye separations60/120/180 reference pixels, reports visible/not-seen/visible, plus both-eyes at120 visible. Reference800px setup is hypothetical, not the current physical screen; data supplied once with prominent source, no pointless transcription of all seven values. Learner still manipulates retinal labels/light explanation and interprets the evidence. Provides a data-analysis route for those not using the perceptual task; actual assistive-technology acceptance separate.

Stage1 original enlarged retinal-layer/disc/nerve schematic: rod/cone photoreceptors absent at optic-disc exit, ganglion output fibers converge and leave as optic nerve. Light-detection absence is not absence of signals traveling through that nerve. Broad schematic, cell types/details omitted. Label placements and two light-marker comparisons at receptor patch versus disc illustrate function. No copied source figures or claim direct rod-to-nerve action potentials. Nasal retina corresponds to temporal visual field; no misleading same-side retina/visual-field drawing. Horizontal profile only, not full2D calibrated perimetry.

Coarse pattern inference uses current confirmed reports: visible at lower and higher separation around at least one not-seen point in a matching context is compatible with a sampled localized gap; otherwise report no clear bracket. Uncertain/no-not-seen/mixed observations accepted, no automatic physiological correctness label. Both-eyes result interpreted only with its actual matching context/side/position; not automatic proof of filling-in or exact mechanism. Knowledge checks explicitly Correct!/Try again; raw self-reports recorded rather than graded. First reasoning, revisions and source distinctions reach completed PDF/native print/JSON.
