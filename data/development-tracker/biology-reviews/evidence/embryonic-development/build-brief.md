# BIO-29-BUILD: Early Embryonic Development

Question: How do cell division and cell movement build an early embryo?

Distinguish more, smaller cells during cleavage from growth of the whole embryo. Evidence: Use your cell count and average relative volume. How did cleavage change cell number and cell size without doubling the whole embryo? What was assumed?

Identify cell ingression and sheet invagination as different gastrulation movements. Evidence: Compare ingression and invagination in the sections. What moved, and where? Why is the remaining blastocoel different from the inside of the early gut pouch?

Relate ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm to tissue origins in a sea-urchin model. Evidence: Use your three tissue labels to explain ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm. Why are these origins not a claim that all organs, animals or stages form three nested rings? Compare the result with your prediction.

Interactions and workload: Follow one original teaching sequence. Use a process control at its work site, then inspect the changed section. These controls step through supplied biology; you are not adding enzymes or physically pushing an embryo. Later cleavage and molecular signals are supplied. One developmental sequence, not three independent embryos or timed measurements. Record the two-cell count and average relative cell volume, then trace two different gastrulation movements and label three distinct future tissue groups.

Model limits: These original drawings are enlarged teaching sections, not photographs, measured embryos or a complete three-dimensional cell map. Relative volume uses a chosen initial total of1and an equal first division, with little net growth. Later divisions can be unequal and cells can keep dividing during gastrulation. Cell number and wall thickness are reduced in the drawings. Real stages overlap and involve many signals, changes in adhesion and cell shape. Some mesoderm cells enter the cavity before gut invagination; more mesoderm populations and later gut extension are supplied or omitted here. The early gut pouch is open at its base and is not yet a complete through-gut. The blastocoel is not the gut lumen. Germ-layer names describe origins, not three permanent nested rings. Other animals, including mammals, develop differently. No human ages, clinical outcomes or live-embryo procedures are modeled.

Design decisions: Sea-urchin sections distinguish cleavage subdivision, individual mesoderm ingression and sheet invagination. Three origin labels interpret supplied fate, not causally transform tissue. One sequence, no default three repeats; original reduced cell counts and chosen relative volume explicit.
