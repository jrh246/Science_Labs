# BIO-33-BUILD: Design a Biodiversity Reserve

Question: How can two reserve layouts protect different habitats and connections under the same budget?

Compare protected habitat areas under an equal budget. Evidence: Compare your two protected habitat-area totals and rationales. What did you gain or give up when changing A to B? Why does equal total area not guarantee the same habitats or species?

Use adjoining groups and reserve boundary length to describe layout tradeoffs. Evidence: Use both group counts and boundary lengths. How did shared sides or gaps change your map? Why are these geometric connections and reserve boundaries different from measured movement and ecological habitat edges?

Justify a conservation choice while separating map metrics from biological outcomes. Evidence: Which plan would you investigate further for your stated goal, and why? Use evidence from both maps, allow ties, compare your prediction, and name two missing kinds of biological or community evidence. Why is your choice not proof of a universally best reserve?

Interactions and workload: The fictional6×6map has woodland, meadow and wetland. Each100m×100mcell is1hectare (ha), or10000m², and costs1fictional planning credit. Each plan must select exactly12cells. Mark land for protection or remove a mark; this changes planning status, not habitat type. Make two different layouts and explain your choices. Plan B starts as a clearly labeled copy of your recorded A layout to save repeated setup; change it before recording. Explanations are not copied. These are design alternatives, not independent field samples. Tied metrics and different defensible goals are allowed.

Model limits: This original map, equal costs and habitat categories are teaching assumptions, not survey data or real land values. Adjoining groups count selected cells connected along a side; diagonal contact does not join groups in this geometric rule. This is not measured animal movement: organisms can cross unprotected land, avoid some selected habitat, or disperse across gaps. Boundary length counts selected sides facing unselected cells or the map’s outside, assumed unselected for this calculation. A reserve boundary is not automatically an ecological habitat edge. Unselected habitat has not been erased or assumed destroyed. Habitat area is not a species count, habitat quality measure or extinction prediction. We omit species ranges, seasonal needs, actual dispersal, enforcement, changing costs, land ownership and community priorities. No layout is automatically best for biodiversity. Real corridor effects and reserve choices require evidence for the organisms and landscape involved.

Design decisions: Two different12-credit/12ha student designs compare habitat protection, side-adjoining groups and reserve-status boundary. B copies only the starting A layout to avoid repetitive setup; independent rationale and changed layout required. Geometry is not measured movement, habitat destruction or biodiversity/survival score; no universal best design. Original terrain and printable reference grid.
