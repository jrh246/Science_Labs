## Learning and science review

Question: Which types of biological molecules can you detect in an unknown sample?

1. Use controls and separate portions: the student records a prediction about whether one indicator can answer a different molecule question and explains positive/negative controls before apparatus/results. This is a reasoned test-design prediction, not an uninformed guess about an unidentified food brand.
2. Classify evidence: students place an unknown portion and the appropriate reagent, complete required treatment, compare actual visible/accessible appearances with supplied controls, and record both observation and molecule claim for four distinct targets.
3. Explain limits: original writing must address different targets, controls, comparison with prediction and limited negative inference. No exact amount or named food identity is inferred. Written work remains ungraded.

Primary methods source read: Pratt (2011), Journal of Microbiology & Biology Education, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3577220/. Institutional teaching cross-check: Hillsborough College laboratory manual, https://pressbooks.hcfl.edu/bio1ss/chapter/lab-5-biological-molecules/. Facts and original model/artwork were authored independently; no source diagrams or worksheet text were copied. Exact virtual sample profiles are synthetic, not measured food composition.

Benedict's is interpreted only after heating and detects reducing sugars, including glucose/lactose, rather than every carbohydrate or sugar. Iodine detects starch. Biuret provides peptide-bond evidence; free amino acids do not give the same modeled positive result. Sudan IV paper staining follows the research paper method: stain, rinse, then inspect retained color. Dye partition/staining is not presented as a protein reaction. Four seeded profiles provide a mix of results; nothing claims that real foods follow those exact profiles.

One unknown is divided into fresh portions because indicators must not contaminate one another and each assay answers a different question. Only the first portion placement is required as preparation practice; later model-prepared matched portions are optional. Each assay still requires actual student treatment/interpretation. Positive/negative reference controls are supplied, not invented student trials. Sample identities/profiles and choices persist across reload; portions are not claimed to be independent biological replicates. Limits include sensitivity, colored/interfering foods, unknown quantities and shortened treatment time. Negative means not detected by that test, not proof of zero or absence of all other molecules.

## Findings and fixes retained

- The initial resource generator lacked its Small paragraph style. Defined it, regenerated resources and verified links/prompts/hashes.
- Separate visual review found sticky tools covering the tube/rinse vessel. A compact nonoverlapping workbench keeps tool choices immediately below the actual scene, and drag tests center that whole workbench.
- The last lipid assay still said to continue to another test. Its status now directs students to combine the four results.
- Complete reports repeated supplied mechanism paragraphs. Replaced them with a compact labeled control/status table, retaining full teaching explanations in the teacher guide and in-app feedback.
- A bath-indicator artwork edit introduced a template syntax error. The build caught it; the nested template was corrected and the full workflow rerun. This failure is distinct from passing model tests.
- A fixed post-drag timeout could ignore a student's next real tool click. Shared touch handling now suppresses only the generated click and resets on the next physical pointerdown; keyboard clicks remain usable. Rapid tool switches, touch taps and existing new labs are regressions.
- Wrong unfinished reports in BIO-01/02 were corrected to retain current entries as unconfirmed. Repeat-correct checks no longer invent corrected learning; focused model/browser/PDF/print evidence is retained separately.

Final actual commands, counts, source hashes and rendered artifacts are recorded in this review's JSON and evidence directory. Passing development checks do not establish classroom comprehension, translated readiness, hardware or full-course approval.
