# Transpiration — individual build brief

Question: How does one chosen environmental change affect modeled plant water loss, and how does water reach the air?

Goals: choose and control one factor; use initial/final mass changes and time to compare rates rather than final masses; locate evaporation at wet mesophyll surfaces and vapor diffusion through stomata. Students choose airflow, humidity or brighter daytime light before recording a required prediction/plan. Compare reference versus changed station only, not all factors per student or triple trials. Other settings fixed:25°C, matched100cm² leaf area and water supply; RH50% except humid80%; baseline relative stomatal/boundary conductances1/1, wind boundary3, brighter-daytime stomatal2. Changes are idealized supplied settings, not calibrated physical fan/lamp/humidifier protocols or universal plant responses.

Two matched virtual stations have separate balances. Place a soil/pot cover around each stem without covering leaves, move the potted plant onto its pan, install the chosen external controller module at the changed station (not on the balance), record both initial masses, run one synchronized30-model-minute interval, read both final masses and calculate losses/rates. Total starting masses differ120/123g to require within-station subtraction; pot/cover baseline differences do not imply unequal leaf area. Both final values must come from the fixed modeled interval; no real-time screen timing or repeated sampling claims.

Physical balancing, airflow force, enclosure regulation and thermal equilibration are idealized. No physical classroom protocol.

Leaf section interaction: place evaporation annotation on a wet mesophyll wall, showing liquid-to-vapor change; diffusion annotation on the stomatal route, showing vapor reaching air. Xylem, air space, guard cells and limited cuticular loss explained with faithful enlarged original art; no liquid droplet exits stomata, guard-cell pumping claim or passive process mislabeled as ATP-driven water transport.
