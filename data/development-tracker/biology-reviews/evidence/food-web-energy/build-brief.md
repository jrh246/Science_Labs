# BIO-32-BUILD: Energy Through a Food Web

Question: Where does food energy go, and how much becomes new tissue at the next feeding level?

Trace food-to-eater energy paths through a branching web. Evidence: Use your four feeding links to trace energy to the hawk along two paths. Why do the arrows point from food to eater? Why is this not a count of individual animals?

Distinguish new tissue, unabsorbed food and respiration in a conserved energy budget. Evidence: Use the grasshopper budget and the supplied final totals: heat315, unabsorbed food155, remaining new living tissue530kJ. Why does this add to1000without energy being destroyed? Why is detrital chemical energy different from heat?

Calculate and compare transfers between level production using matched area and time. Evidence: Show both level-transfer calculations using production1000,50and5kJ. Why do their percentages differ from one another and from a consumer’s production divided by its own ingested food? Compare your prediction and name two model limits.

Interactions and workload: Build the four feeding links in one generalized web using food-energy tracers at the eater’s mouth. These are teaching tracers, not real feeding tools or whole-animal counts. Trace one consumer budget in detail; later consumer budgets are supplied. One invented web and matched-area/time energy budget, not repeated ecosystem trials. Different feeding branches and two different level transfers test distinct ideas. A kJ is a kilojoule, a unit of energy. All values refer to the same1m²area and one chosen model period, not measured field rates.

Model limits: Original drawings, food links and energy values are teaching assumptions, not photographs, empirical species diets or universal rates. Organism size and arrow width do not encode biomass, abundance or energy amount. Start with1000kJ of net plant production after plant respiration; sunlight input and plant respiration lie outside this budget. Only new production from this period is followed. Some remains uneaten in living tissue at the end; it is not all standing biomass. Unabsorbed food remains chemical energy in the detrital path, which includes waste and dead material. Later decomposition, death, other excretion and exports are not simulated. Decomposers can use detrital chemical energy and release heat; nutrients cycle, but heat is not recycled as food energy. Respired energy is not destroyed. The chosen5percent and10percent transfers are not constants for every ecosystem or a prediction of animal population size.

Design decisions: Four branched food-energy links at actual mouths, one detailed conserved consumer budget and supplied later budgets; two production-to-production level transfers5%and10%are distinct, not universal. Chemical fecal/live tissue energy versus eventual respiration heat, no double-counted level inputs; matched area/time and original toy boundary explicit.
