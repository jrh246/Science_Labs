# BIO-31-BUILD: Estimating Population Size

Question: How can marked animals help estimate a hidden population, and why can estimates differ?

Use marked fraction and a counted recapture to calculate an estimate. Evidence: Use one of your catches to explain M, n, R and M×n/R. How does a marked fraction lead to an estimated population rather than an exact count?

Evidence: Compare your three random catches and estimates, including ties or any zero recaptures. What stayed fixed? Compare with the revealed model count and your prediction. Why are these not independent ponds?

Explain how unequal catching and zero recaptures limit the simple ratio estimate. Evidence: Use the supplied low-recapture case to explain what happens when marked fish are underrepresented. Name two other assumptions, explain R=0, and describe evidence needed before trusting a real survey.

Interactions and workload: Catch20fish, apply virtual marks to the batch once, then release them. Mixing is supplied. Keep that original marked group unchanged. Draw three fresh20-fish recapture samples, releasing all fish and supplying remixing between them. Each fish is chosen at most once within a catch but may appear in later catches. Three fresh random catches from one fixed population show sampling variation. They are not three independent ponds. A separate supplied low-recapture example tests a changed catching assumption without repeating all preparation.

Model limits: This is a seeded finite-population teaching model, not a field survey. In the equal-catch cases, every subset of20fish is equally likely; the same20original fish remain marked, all marks are retained/read and no fish enter, leave, die or reproduce. Mixing is an assumption, not a timing rule for real fish. The simple estimate is M×n/R, where M is marked first, n is caught later and R is marked in that later catch. It is a rough ratio estimate, not an exact count or a guaranteed unbiased method. If R=0, this formula has no finite estimate; zero recaptures does not mean zero fish. Small recapture numbers can give unstable answers. Other estimators, such as Chapman’s correction, and uncertainty intervals are outside this lesson. The supplied low-recapture case is a designed illustration, not a measured trap response or a random paired experiment. Drawings enlarge fish, show captured animals only and do not reveal the whole pond.

Design decisions: One marked batch, release/remix supplied, three genuinely fresh finite-population recaptures show variation.
